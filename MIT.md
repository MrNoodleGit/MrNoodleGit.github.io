### The undergraduate population is unusually intellectually strong

MIT’s Class of 2029 had **29,281 applicants and 1,334 admits—4.6%**. Among enrolled students, the middle 50% of admitted students scored **780–800 on SAT Math** and **740–780 on SAT Evidence-Based Reading and Writing**.

But test scores actually understate the difference. MIT explicitly selects for things like **curiosity, initiative, hands-on creativity, risk-taking, intensity, and collaborative ability**, rather than simply selecting the highest standardized-test scorers.

A typical MIT student isn't merely someone who can get an A in calculus. They're often someone who, at 17 or 19, has already spent years obsessing over something unusually difficult—building robots, doing mathematical research, making software, studying physics independently, starting a company, competing in olympiads, etc.

### The really striking thing is the _density_

At a normal university, one can be one of the smartest people in a room.

At MIT, one can walk into a room where **almost everyone is exceptionally capable in some dimension**.

And that's psychologically different.

One might encounter:

- Someone who can casually do mathematics that others find extremely difficult.
- Someone who has already published research.
- Someone who can build a functioning electronic system from scratch.
- Someone whose knowledge of physics makes undergraduate coursework look introductory.
- Someone who is simultaneously a great programmer, musician, entrepreneur, and athlete.
- Someone who seems totally ordinary until it becomes clear that they've been quietly working on an absurdly difficult problem for three years.

And then one realizes they're the _normal person in that particular room_.

**raw intelligence isn't necessarily the most impressive characteristic of the MIT environment.**

The unusual combination is:

> **high cognitive ability + extreme curiosity + willingness to work on hard things for a long time.**

That combination produces a lot of the spectacular outcomes associated with MIT.

### And the faculty are on another level

This is where the comparison becomes almost absurd.

MIT currently counts **106 Nobel Prizes, 85 MacArthur Fellows, 65 National Medals of Science, 35 National Medals of Technology and Innovation, and 18 Turing Awards** among its community's honors.

In **2026**, two MIT-trained mathematicians, Yu Deng and Hong Wang, were among the four recipients of the **Fields Medal**, the highest honor in mathematics.

So there are professors at MIT whose work is literally at the frontier of human knowledge.

### The part people outside MIT often don't understand

Being surrounded by very intelligent people changes one's reference frame.

Someone who was the smartest kid in their high school can arrive at MIT and suddenly become the person struggling to understand something that their roommate finds obvious.

That can be humbling.

Conversely, someone who felt mediocre elsewhere can discover that they're actually extremely capable when surrounded by people who think the way they do.

So one could describe MIT as:

> **“One of the highest-density environments in the world for unusually capable, curious, technically sophisticated young people.”**
